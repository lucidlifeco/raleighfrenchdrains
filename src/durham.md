# French Drains and Yard Drainage in Durham

Soggy backyards, water pooling against the foundation, or runoff from the lot uphill: Durham yards see all of it. Talk to a local drainage pro who works in Durham: French drains, regrading, catch basins, downspout lines and dry wells. One call gets you started.

## The drainage on your lot is yours

Durham's rule is plain. The city code states that maintenance of drainage systems, both surface and underground, located on private property is the responsibility of the property owner.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> That includes pipes that cross your yard, even when the City owns the section that runs up to your property line.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup>

The City maintains the stormwater system in its rights-of-way. Outside the city limits, Durham County gives the same general answer: the owner of the property where a drainage system sits is responsible for maintaining it.<sup><a href="#s2">2</a></sup>

## The City's drainage assistance program

Durham does something most Triangle towns don't. Its stormwater code includes a **private drainage assistance hardship program** that owners of single-family residential parcels can apply for.<sup><a href="#s3">3</a></sup> The City's Drainage and Floodplain Unit investigates private drainage and flooding inquiries and processes these requests.<sup><a href="#s4">4</a></sup>

It's worth asking about if a failing pipe or channel on your lot carries water from beyond your property. But requests are ranked by priority category,<sup><a href="#s3">3</a></sup> so it isn't a quick fix. For a soggy yard, a low spot or downspout water, the faster path is a local drainage pro.

Questions for the City go to Durham One Call at (919) 560-1200.<sup><a href="#s5">5</a></sup>

## When the water comes from next door

North Carolina's reasonable use rule lets you change how water drains on your land, but you can be liable if the change causes unreasonable harm next door.<sup><a href="#s6">6</a></sup> Good fixes move water where it naturally wants to go, on your own lot:

- A **[swale](/yard-grading-swales/)** that steers incoming water around the house
- A **[French drain](/french-drain-installation/)** to catch water soaking through from uphill
- A **[dry well](/dry-wells/)** when there's no lower spot on your lot for the water

## What drainage work costs

- **Yard French drains** in the Triangle typically cost **$20 to $50 per linear foot**, or about $1,970 to $6,880 for a typical project.<sup><a href="#s7">7</a></sup>
- See the [French drain cost guide](/french-drain-cost-raleigh/) for prices by length and how other fixes compare.

## Before anyone digs

- **Call 811** at least three full working days before digging so utility lines get marked.<sup><a href="#s8">8</a></sup>
- **Check your plat and the City's GIS** for drainage easements and pipes on your lot.
- **Ask where the water will end up.** A good drainage pro will show you the outlet before work starts.
