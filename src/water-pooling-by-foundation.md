# Water Pooling by Your Foundation

Water that sits against your foundation is the drainage problem to fix first. It soaks the soil under the house, finds its way into crawl spaces, and keeps the area around your footings wet long after the rest of the yard dries out.

## The slope your yard should have

The International Residential Code, which North Carolina's residential code is based on,<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> calls for the ground to fall **at least 6 inches within the first 10 feet** from the foundation.<sup><a href="#s2">2</a></sup> Where a lot line or wall makes that impossible, drains or swales should carry water away instead, and patios, walks, and driveways within 10 feet of the house should slope away at **at least 2 percent**.<sup><a href="#s3">3</a></sup>

Many homes met that standard when they were built and drifted out of it. Soil settles along the foundation, mulch beds get built up, and new patios get poured flat.

**A quick check:** lay a straight 10-foot board on the ground, one end against the foundation, and put a level on it. The far end should sit about 6 inches lower than the end at the house.

{% figure "grading-and-swale.svg", "Ground sloping at least 6 inches away from a house in the first 10 feet", "The ground should drop about 6 inches in the first 10 feet from your foundation." %}

## Common causes

- **Downspouts emptying right at the foundation.** Usually the biggest single source. See [underground downspout lines](/underground-downspouts/).
- **Settled or reversed grade** next to the house. See [regrading and swales](/yard-grading-swales/).
- **A patio, walkway, or driveway sloping toward the house**
- **Water from uphill** soaking through the soil toward the house. A [French drain](/french-drain-installation/) between the slope and the house can intercept it.
- **Clogged or overflowing gutters** dumping water along the whole roofline

## Fixes, from simplest to biggest

1. **Clean gutters and extend downspouts** at least several feet from the house.
2. **Bury downspout lines** so roof water ends up well away from the foundation. Typical cost: $200 to $2,000 per downspout.<sup><a href="#s4">4</a></sup>
3. **Regrade along the foundation** so the ground slopes away. Fixing grading around a house typically costs $500 to $3,000.<sup><a href="#s5">5</a></sup>
4. **Install an exterior French drain** to intercept water before it reaches the house. Raleigh yard French drains run about $20 to $50 per foot.<sup><a href="#s6">6</a></sup>
5. **Interior or crawl space drainage** when water is already getting in. These cost more: about $40 to $85 per foot for an interior basement drain and $20 to $40 per foot for a crawl space drain, nationally.<sup><a href="#s7">7</a></sup>

Most homes need one of the first three. A good drainage pro will start with the cheapest fix that actually solves the problem.

## When to get help soon

- Water in the crawl space after storms
- Musty smells or damp insulation under the house
- New cracks, or doors and windows that start sticking

These can point to bigger issues, so have the drainage looked at, and consider a foundation or crawl space specialist if water is already getting in.
