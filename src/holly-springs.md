# French Drains and Yard Drainage in Holly Springs

New neighborhoods, graded lots and backyards that slope toward each other: Holly Springs has plenty of yards where water collects after a storm. Talk to a local drainage pro who works in Holly Springs: French drains, regrading, catch basins, downspout lines and dry wells. One call gets you started.

## Lot drainage is a private matter here

Holly Springs is direct about this. The Town says it does not regulate drainage from private lots, and storm drainage issues between property owners are a civil matter.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> It does ask residents making landscape changes that affect water flow to take their neighbor's property into consideration.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup>

North Carolina law backs that up. Under the reasonable use rule, you can change how water drains on your land, but you can be liable if the change causes unreasonable harm next door.<sup><a href="#s2">2</a></sup> So a good fix moves water where it naturally wants to go, on your own lot, without dumping it on the house downhill.

## Check your plat for drainage easements

Many Holly Springs lots have drainage easements, strips of land kept open so stormwater can flow through. Your recorded plat shows the easements that apply to your property.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup>

- **Most are private.** Unless the plat says otherwise, drainage easements outside the right-of-way are private, and the Town does not maintain them.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup>
- **No structures.** The Town will not grant permission to install structures on these easements. Fences and landscaping may be allowed at the discretion of whoever the easement serves, such as your HOA.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup>

If water backs up in an easement behind your house, clearing and grading it is usually up to the owners and the HOA, not the Town. A [swale](/yard-grading-swales/) that keeps the flow path open is often the right fix.

## Watch your impervious limit

This rule surprises people planning a patio or hardscape as part of a drainage project. Holly Springs sets impervious surface limits through each development's stormwater plan, lists them on the plat, and will not approve exceeding them.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> Permeable pavers on a private lot don't get an exemption unless a stormwater management plan is submitted and approved.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup>

Drainage fixes that don't add hard surface, like a [French drain](/french-drain-installation/), a swale, or [underground downspout lines](/underground-downspouts/) to a pop-up emitter, avoid the issue.

## What drainage work costs

- **Yard French drains** in the Raleigh area typically cost **$20 to $50 per linear foot**, or about $1,970 to $6,880 for a typical project.<sup><a href="#s3">3</a></sup>
- See the [French drain cost guide](/french-drain-cost-raleigh/) for prices by length and how swales, catch basins and dry wells compare.

## Before anyone digs

- **Call 811.** The Town directs residents to NC 811 before digging,<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> and state law requires notice at least three full working days ahead.<sup><a href="#s4">4</a></sup>
- **Control erosion on your lot.** The Town requires that any land disturbance causing sediment or erosion be controlled on site.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup>
- **Questions for the Town** go to Holly Springs Development Services at 311, or (919) 577-3111 from outside town limits.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup>
