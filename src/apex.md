## Who's responsible for drainage in Apex

**Private property is on you.** The Town of Apex maintains drainage only in public easements and within public roads and rights-of-way. Drainage systems on private property are the owner's responsibility.<sup><a href="#s3">3</a></sup>

**The stormwater fee doesn't cover your yard.** Apex began charging a stormwater utility fee on January 1, 2022, and it appears on your monthly utility bill. The Town notes that the fee does not cover drainage issues or flooding on private property.<sup><a href="#s4">4</a></sup>

## Where your drain can empty in Apex

This is the part many homeowners don't know. Apex's drainage standards say private piped storm drainage systems may not cross property lines, carry stormwater from one lot to another except in specific approved cases, or point-discharge next to the curb.<sup><a href="#s5">5</a></sup>

What that means for a French drain or buried downspout line:

- Plan an outlet on your own property, such as a pop-up emitter, a [dry well](/dry-wells/), or a spot where water can spread out on lower ground.
- Don't plan to pipe water into a neighbor's yard or straight to the curb.
- Ask your drainage pro how they'll handle the outlet before work starts. If they're unsure, check with the Town.

## Apex's own advice

The Town's stormwater tips encourage residents to make sure water drains away from the house to protect the foundation, and to consider turning naturally wet areas of the yard into rain gardens with appropriate plants.<sup><a href="#s6">6</a></sup> A rain garden can be a good fit for a soggy spot that doesn't threaten the house.

When raking leaves to the curb for pickup, the Town asks residents to keep yard waste at least 10 feet away from storm drain inlets, because leaves clog drains and pipes and cause flooding.<sup><a href="#s6">6</a></sup>
