# Yard Regrading and Swales in Raleigh

Sometimes the problem isn't missing pipe. It's the shape of the ground. If your yard slopes toward the house, or water sheets across the lawn and collects in the same place every storm, reshaping the ground is often the simplest and cheapest fix.

## Regrading vs. a swale

**Regrading** reshapes the soil so water flows away from the house. It's the go-to fix when the ground next to the foundation is flat or slopes the wrong way.

**A swale** is a shallow, grassy channel that steers surface water around a problem area and toward a lower spot. Swales work well along property lines and at the bottom of slopes.

Neither fix needs buried pipe, so both are easy to maintain. They handle water moving across the surface. For ground that stays saturated, a [French drain](/french-drain-installation/) is usually the better tool.

## The 6-inch rule around your foundation

The International Residential Code, which North Carolina's residential code is based on,<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> says the ground should fall **at least 6 inches within the first 10 feet** of the foundation.<sup><a href="#s2">2</a></sup> When a lot line, wall, or slope makes that impossible, the code calls for drains or swales to carry water away instead, and paved surfaces within 10 feet of the house should slope away at **at least 2 percent**.<sup><a href="#s3">3</a></sup>

Over time, settling soil, new mulch beds, and patios can flatten or reverse that slope. A quick check with a 10-foot board and a level shows whether yours still drains away.

## What it costs in Raleigh

- **Minor grading or resloping in Raleigh** averages about **$1,895**, with most jobs between $844 and $3,041.<sup><a href="#s4">4</a></sup>
- **A swale** typically runs **$5 to $15 per foot**, or about $790 to $2,950 total in Raleigh.<sup><a href="#s5">5</a></sup>
- **Fixing the grade around a house** costs about $500 to $3,000. Larger areas run $0.40 to $1 per square foot for fine grading and $1 to $2 per square foot for rough grading.<sup><a href="#s6">6</a></sup>
- **Materials:** topsoil runs about $15 to $55 per cubic yard and sod about $0.35 to $0.85 per square foot in Raleigh.<sup><a href="#s7">7</a></sup>

## Before you change the grade

- **Don't push water onto a neighbor.** North Carolina's reasonable use rule lets you change how water drains on your land, but you can be liable if the change causes unreasonable harm next door.<sup><a href="#s8">8</a></sup>
- **Check easements and your HOA.** Many subdivisions have recorded drainage easements, and HOA rules may control grading and landscaping.
- **Call 811 before digging** to get utility lines marked.
