# Dry Well Installation in Raleigh

Every drainage fix needs somewhere to send the water. When there's no lower ground, ditch, or street within reach, a dry well can be the answer: a buried pit that holds runoff and lets it soak into the ground slowly.

## How a dry well works

A dry well is a hole filled with gravel or fitted with a perforated plastic tank, wrapped in filter fabric and covered with soil and sod. Pipes from a [French drain](/french-drain-installation/), [catch basin](/catch-basins/), or [downspouts](/underground-downspouts/) feed into it. During a storm the dry well fills, and over the following hours the water seeps out into the surrounding soil.

## The catch in Raleigh: clay

A dry well only works if the soil around it can absorb water. Much of the Raleigh area sits on red clay. Cecil soil, North Carolina's state soil, has a firm, sticky clay subsoil.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> In tight clay, a dry well can stay full for a long time and back up during the next storm.

That doesn't rule dry wells out. It means sizing matters, and so does testing. Ask your contractor to test how fast your soil drains where the dry well would go (a percolation test) before you commit. A larger dry well, or one dug deeper into sandier layers, may be needed.

## When a dry well makes sense

- There's no legal outlet to daylight, a ditch, or the street
- Sending water toward the street would mean crossing a neighbor's property
- Downspout water needs to go somewhere other than the foundation
- The soil tests show it absorbs water at a workable rate

## What it costs in Raleigh

A dry well typically costs **$985 to $3,930** installed in Raleigh.<sup><a href="#s2">2</a></sup> Size, depth, and how much digging the soil takes drive the price. See the [Raleigh French drain cost guide](/french-drain-cost-raleigh/) to compare dry wells with other drainage fixes.

## Before installation

- **Call 811** at least three full working days before digging.<sup><a href="#s3">3</a></sup>
- **Keep the dry well away from the house**, so the water soaking out doesn't head back toward the foundation.
- **Plan for overflow.** Ask what happens when a big storm fills the dry well faster than it drains.
