# French Drain Cost in Raleigh, NC (2026)

## What drives the price in Raleigh

**Length and depth.** Labor and digging are most of the bill. A shallow drain across the yard is the cheapest per foot. A deep drain along a foundation costs much more.<sup><a href="#s3">3</a></sup>

**Red clay.** Cecil soil, North Carolina's official state soil, has a red clay subsoil that's firm and sticky.<sup><a href="#s5">5</a></sup> On many newer lots, grading strips away topsoil and leaves packed clay near the surface. That clay can be slow to absorb a heavy rain, and it's harder work to dig.

**Where the water goes.** Every drain needs a lower outlet, such as the street, a ditch, a dry well or a pop-up emitter in the lawn. If the nearest good outlet is far away, the drain gets longer and the price goes up.

**Materials.** Gravel runs about $30 to $60 per ton in Raleigh,<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> and drain pipe costs roughly $0.70 to $3 per foot.<sup><a href="#s6">6</a></sup> Materials are a small part of the total. Labor is the big part.

**Putting the yard back.** Ask whether the quote includes replacing sod, mulch, or plants along the trench line. Many don't.

**Rain.** Raleigh gets rain in every month, and September is the wettest at about 5.1 inches on average.<sup><a href="#s7">7</a></sup> Contractors tend to book up after big storms, so it pays to plan before the wet season.

## Rules to know before anyone digs

**Call 811 first.** North Carolina law requires notice to NC 811 at least three full working days before digging, so utility lines can be marked. It's free. Dial 811 or 1-800-632-4949.<sup><a href="#s8">8</a></sup> A good contractor handles this for you, but ask.

**Don't send the problem next door.** North Carolina follows a "reasonable use" rule for surface water. You can change how water drains on your property, but you can be liable if the change causes unreasonable harm to a neighbor.<sup><a href="#s9">9</a></sup> Plan the outlet with that in mind.

**Check the outlet.** Tying a drain into a street, ditch, or storm drain may need approval from your town or HOA. Ask your contractor to confirm before work starts.

## DIY or hire a pro?

Doing it yourself saves less than most people expect. One cost guide priced a 25-foot DIY French drain at about $528, or roughly $21 per foot.<sup><a href="#s10">10</a></sup> That's already inside the $20 to $50 per foot range Raleigh pros charge,<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> before you count the digging.

DIY can make sense for a short, shallow run with an obvious place for the water to go. Hire a pro when the drain runs near the foundation, needs to be deep, crosses buried utilities, or when there's no clear outlet.

## How to get an accurate quote

- Ask for an on-site visit. Have the contractor show you where the water collects, the route of the drain and the outlet.
- Get the length, depth, price per foot and total in writing.
- Ask what's included: gravel, filter fabric, pipe type, catch basins, cleanouts, and lawn repair.
- Ask how they'll handle the outlet and whether a permit or HOA approval is needed.
- Ask for proof of insurance and any license that applies, and what the warranty covers.
- Get two quotes when you can.
