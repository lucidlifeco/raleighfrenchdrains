# French Drains and Yard Drainage in Wake Forest

Water standing in the backyard after every storm, a soggy side yard, or runoff washing out beds: if that's your Wake Forest yard, it's usually fixable. We connect Wake Forest homeowners with local drainage pros for French drains, regrading, catch basins, downspout lines, and dry wells.

## Where the Town's responsibility ends

Wake Forest draws a clear line in its development ordinance. Drainage easements are public only to the end of a storm drainage pipe system. Past that point, drainage runs through private easements owned and maintained by the individual property owner, both inside town limits and in the ETJ.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup>

The ordinance also lists what the Town won't maintain: drainage systems on private property without dedicated easements, drainage maintained by NCDOT as part of the state highway system, and private detention or water quality ponds.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup>

In plain terms: if the water problem is in your yard and not in a public pipe or street, the fix is usually yours to make.

## What the stormwater fee pays for

Wake Forest's stormwater utility fee went into effect July 1, 2024. It helps fund the management, maintenance and improvement of the Town's stormwater infrastructure and drainage systems, including a Public Works stormwater crew and capital projects.<sup><a href="#s2">2</a></sup> Homes in the Franklin County part of town are billed by the Town as well.<sup><a href="#s2">2</a></sup>

The fee pays for the Town's system. It doesn't mean the Town will regrade your yard or install a drain on your lot. If you're unsure whether a problem involves Town infrastructure, the Town's stormwater utility manager can be reached at (919) 435-9449.<sup><a href="#s2">2</a></sup>

## When the water comes from next door

Runoff from a neighbor's yard is one of the most common complaints, and it's the one the Town is least likely to step into. Wake Forest's ordinance says the Town assumes no liability or responsibility for settling disputes between property owners over stormwater that isn't publicly generated.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup>

That makes your own drainage plan matter more. North Carolina's reasonable use rule lets you change how water drains on your land, but you can be liable if the change causes unreasonable harm next door.<sup><a href="#s3">3</a></sup> Good options:

- A **[swale](/yard-grading-swales/)** along the property line that steers incoming water around the house
- A **[French drain](/french-drain-installation/)** at the bottom of the slope to catch water soaking through from uphill
- A **[dry well](/dry-wells/)** when there's no lower ground on your lot to send water to

## What drainage work costs

- **Yard French drains** in the Raleigh area typically cost **$20 to $50 per linear foot**, or about $1,970 to $6,880 for a typical project.<sup><a href="#s4">4</a></sup>
- See the [French drain cost guide](/french-drain-cost-raleigh/) for prices by length and how swales, catch basins and dry wells compare.

## Before anyone digs

- **Call 811** at least three full working days before digging so utility lines get marked.<sup><a href="#s5">5</a></sup>
- **Check your plat for drainage easements.** If a private drainage easement crosses your yard, plan work so water can still flow through it.
- **Ask where the water will end up.** A good drainage pro will show you the outlet before work starts.
