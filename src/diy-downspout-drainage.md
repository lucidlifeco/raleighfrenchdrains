# DIY Downspout Drainage vs. Hiring a Pro

Downspouts are the one drainage problem where some real fixes are true DIY jobs. Others look easy and turn into a weekend of trenching. Here's where the line is.

## Fixes you can do this weekend

**Downspout extensions.** Most above-ground extensions cost $5 to $20 each.<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup> Department of Energy guidance calls for downspouts to discharge at least 5 feet from the foundation onto the surface.<sup><a href="#s2">2</a></sup> Make sure the ground where it lets out slopes away from the house.

**Splash blocks.** They cost $5 to $15<sup><a href="#s1">1</a></sup>, but only move water about 2 feet. They're enough only where the ground already drops sharply away from the house.<sup><a href="#s3">3</a></sup> In Raleigh clay, that's rarely the whole fix.

**Clean the gutters.** Clogged gutters overflow at the house no matter where the downspouts end.

{% figure "downspout-rock-bed.webp", "A downspout emptying onto a rock bed beside a wall, with a green watering can", "A rock bed stops erosion, but the water still lands next to the house.", 800, 876 %}

## The step up: burying a line

Above-ground extensions get kicked, mowed over and moved. The next step is burying the pipe, and this is where DIY gets harder.

- **A simple buried extension** from one downspout takes about 3 hours and $25 to $50 in parts, according to Angi.<sup><a href="#s4">4</a></sup>
- **A full system** with a catch basin, solid pipe and a pop-up emitter is a different job. One drainage manufacturer estimates 10 to 12 total hours of labor and $395 to $475 in materials for its high-capacity downspout kit.<sup><a href="#s5">5</a></sup>
- **Distance.** Underground systems should carry water at least 10 feet from the foundation.<sup><a href="#s2">2</a></sup>
- **Elevation.** The pop-up emitter has to sit lower than where the water enters, or the system won't drain.<sup><a href="#s5">5</a></sup>
- **811.** North Carolina law requires calling NC 811 at least three full working days before you dig.<sup><a href="#s6">6</a></sup>

## When to call a pro

- Several downspouts need to tie into one line
- The run has to cross a driveway or sidewalk, which means cutting concrete or boring underneath<sup><a href="#s8">8</a></sup>
- You want the water to go to the curb. Coring a curb typically costs $350 to $600, and the town has to allow it.<sup><a href="#s8">8</a></sup>
- The yard is flat, so there's no lower spot for an emitter
- Water is already getting into the crawl space or against the foundation

If the extensions didn't fix it, the problem is usually bigger than the downspouts: grading, a low spot, or water coming from the yard. A local pro can sort that out on one visit.
