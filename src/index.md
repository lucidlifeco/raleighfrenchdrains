# French Drains and Yard Drainage in Raleigh

Standing water after every storm. A soggy patch that never dries out. Water creeping toward the foundation or under the deck. These problems are common in Raleigh-area yards, and a French drain or another drainage fix usually solves them.

## Signs your yard needs drainage work

- Puddles that are still there a day after the rain stops
- Water pooling against the foundation, under the deck, or by the patio
- Mushy lawn, moss, or bare spots in low areas
- Mulch or soil washing across walkways and driveways
- Downspouts dumping water right next to the house
- A damp crawl space or a musty smell under the house

## Common fixes

**[French drain](/french-drain-installation/).** A gravel-filled trench with a perforated pipe that collects water underground and carries it to a lower spot. The go-to fix for a yard that stays wet.

**[Swale or regrading](/yard-grading-swales/).** Reshaping the ground so water flows away from the house instead of toward it. Often the cheapest fix when the slope is the problem.

**[Catch basin](/catch-basins/).** A grated box set at the low spot where water collects, piped to an outlet.

**[Underground downspouts](/underground-downspouts/).** Pipes that carry roof water away from the foundation instead of dumping it beside it.

**[Dry well](/dry-wells/).** A buried pit that holds runoff and lets it soak into the ground slowly, for yards with no good outlet.

A good contractor will tell you which of these your yard actually needs. Sometimes it's a combination.

## Why Raleigh yards hold water

Much of the Raleigh area sits on red clay. Cecil soil, North Carolina's state soil, has a red clay subsoil that's firm and sticky ([USDA](https://soilseries.sc.egov.usda.gov/OSD_Docs/C/Cecil.html)). When building a house strips the topsoil and packs that clay down, rain has a harder time soaking in. Add steady rain all year, with September the wettest month at about 5.1 inches on average ([NOAA data](https://www.extremeweatherwatch.com/cities/raleigh/average-rainfall-by-month)), and low spots stay wet.
